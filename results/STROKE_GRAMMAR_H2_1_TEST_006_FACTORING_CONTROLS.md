Stroke Grammar H2.1 — test 006: matched arbitrary factorization controls

Purpose: determine whether the H2.1 whole-corpus gain is specific to the externally/visually motivated internal factorization or merely a benefit of adding hierarchical model structure.

Real H2.1 held-out NLL per original EVA character: 1.415312498.

GALLOWS CONTROL
All 24 labelled assignments of t/k/p/f to a 2x2 binary component grid were evaluated with the same five whole-folio folds and identical hierarchical model capacity. Pedestal and ordinary gallows used the same assignment in each control.

Result:
- 8/24 assignments had lower NLL than the aaa-motivated assignment.
- 8/24 were numerically equivalent to the aaa assignment.
- best alternative NLL 1.412883366.
- real aaa assignment rank by strict lower-NLL alternatives: 9th.

Interpretation: the global hierarchical likelihood does NOT uniquely select the aaa q/l x p/x gallows geometry. Therefore the H2.1 global gain cannot by itself be cited as evidence that this exact gallows pairing is uniquely correct. The earlier family-level held-out factorization results remain evidence that component factorization is economical, but alternative 2x2 partitions can be equally or more economical globally.

MINIM-TAIL CONTROL
120 deterministic permutations broke the mapping between complete minim-tail forms and their minim-count + tail component labels while retaining the same hierarchical architecture and component-value inventory.

Result:
- real NLL 1.415312498
- control mean NLL 1.426468425
- only 6/120 controls had lower NLL than the real factorization
- best control 1.413864329
- empirical strict-better fraction 0.05; with +1 correction, p-like tail = 7/121 = 0.05785.

Interpretation: the genuine minim-count + tail mapping lies near the favorable edge of the arbitrary-control distribution and is substantially better than the average arbitrary remapping, but it is not uniquely optimal in this 120-control sample.

Overall conclusion:
The matched controls weaken the strongest version of H2.1. Hierarchical factorization clearly improves over flat EVA under the frozen model, but the global improvement is not fully specific to the exact externally motivated component assignments. Evidence is strongest for the minim-count + tail decomposition; exact gallows q/l x p/x geometry is underdetermined by this global likelihood test.

Do not claim that test 006 proves a unique general stroke grammar. Next tests should ask which component relations replicate under independent local/contextual predictions and visual constraints, rather than optimizing global likelihood.

No semantic, phonetic, language, cipher, or historical-origin inference.
